## 1. Executive Summary

The UK Developing Countries Trading Scheme (DCTS) offers zero-tariff access to UK markets for goods from 65 developing countries, representing billions in potential tariff savings annually.

### Key Benefits:

- 0% tariffs on thousands of products
- Simplified rules of origin vs other FTAs
- Cumulation across country groups
- Enhanced access for Least Developed Countries (LDCs)

### Who This Guide is For:

- Exporters in DCTS-eligible countries shipping to the UK
- UK importers sourcing from developing markets
- Freight forwarders serving DCTS corridors
- Customs brokers handling preferential trade

## 2. What is DCTS?

### Overview

The Developing Countries Trading Scheme (DCTS) is the UK's preferential tariff scheme for developing countries, launched in June 2023 following Brexit.

### Background:

- Replaced the EU's Generalised Scheme of Preferences (GSP)
- More generous than previous EU scheme
- Covers 65 countries across Africa, Asia, Caribbean, and Pacific
- Part of UK's independent trade policy

### Three Tiers:

#### Enhanced Framework (24 LDCs)

- Most generous treatment
- Zero tariffs on all products except arms
- Most liberal rules of origin
- **Countries:** Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Ethiopia, Madagascar, Rwanda, Senegal, etc.

#### Comprehensive Framework (19 countries)

- Zero tariffs on most products
- Moderate rules of origin requirements
- **Countries:** Bolivia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Kenya, Pakistan, Philippines, Vietnam, etc.

#### Standard Framework (22 countries)

- Reduced tariffs (not always zero)
- Standard rules of origin
- **Countries:** India, Indonesia, Nigeria, Sri Lanka, etc.

## 4. How DCTS Works

### Tariff Elimination

#### What Gets Zero Tariffs:

- **Enhanced Framework:** 99.9% of tariff lines at 0% (Exceptions: Arms only)
- **Comprehensive Framework:** ~85% of tariff lines at 0%. Reduced tariffs on sensitive sectors. Key sectors duty-free: textiles, electronics, machinery, agriculture.
- **Standard Framework:** ~66% of tariff lines at 0% or reduced. Some products retain MFN rates.

### Comparison: DCTS vs MFN (Most Favoured Nation) Tariffs

| Bicycle parts    | 8714.99 | 4.7%     | 0%                   | £470                |

A Bangladesh garment exporter shipping £500,000/year to UK saves £60,000 annually under DCTS vs MFN rates.

## 5. Eligibility Requirements

### Product Eligibility

Check if Your Product Qualifies:

1. **Verify Origin Country:** Product must originate in DCTS country.
2. **Check Tariff Schedule:** Look up HS code (6-10 digits). Check UK Global Tariff. Verify DCTS preference applies.
3. **Confirm Tier:** Different tiers = different product coverage.

**Example:**

- **Product:** Women's cotton trousers
- **HS Code:** 6204.62.00
- **Origin:** Bangladesh (Enhanced Framework)
- **MFN Rate:** 12%
- **DCTS Rate:** 0%
- ✅ **Qualifies for DCTS**

### Direct Consignment Rule

Goods must be:

- Transported directly from DCTS country to UK, **OR**
- Transit through non-DCTS countries allowed **IF**:
  - Goods remain under customs control
  - No processing occurs
  - Only unloading/reloading permitted

| Allowed ✅                            | Not Allowed ❌                          |
| :------------------------------------ | :-------------------------------------- |
| Bangladesh → Singapore (transit) → UK | Vietnam → China (processing) → UK       |
| Vietnam → Dubai (consolidation) → UK  | Kenya → UAE (repacking/relabeling) → UK |

## 6. Rules of Origin

### What "Origin" Means

Products qualify if they are:

#### A) Wholly Obtained

- Grown, extracted, or produced entirely in DCTS country.
- **Examples:** Agricultural products, minerals, fish caught in territorial waters.

#### B) Sufficiently Transformed

- Made from imported materials BUT sufficient work done to change tariff classification.
- Must meet "change in tariff classification" test.

### Sufficient Transformation Tests

#### Primary Test: Change in Tariff Classification (CTC)

Product must undergo tariff shift:

- 4-digit change (chapter change), OR
- 6-digit change (heading change)

**Example - Cotton T-shirts:**

- **Input:** Cotton fabric (HS 5208)
- **Output:** T-shirts (HS 6109)
- **Change:** ✅ Chapter change (52 → 61)
- **Qualifies as originating**

#### Alternative Tests (Product-Specific)

Some products have specific rules:

- **Value-Added Test:** Minimum % of value must be added in DCTS country (typically 40-50%).
- **Technical Requirement:** Specific manufacturing processes required (e.g., yarn must be spun in DCTS country for textiles).

### Cumulation Rules

#### What is Cumulation?

Using materials from multiple DCTS countries counts as originating.

#### Regional Cumulation Groups:

- **Group 1: Africa:** All African DCTS countries can cumulate.
- **Group 2: ASEAN:** Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Myanmar, Philippines, Vietnam.
- **Group 3: SAARC:** Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan, Sri Lanka.

**Example:**

- Fabric from India (SAARC)
- Cut and sewn in Bangladesh (SAARC)
- Exported to UK
- ✅ **Qualifies** (cumulation within SAARC group)

## 7. Documentation Requirements

### Certificate of Origin

**Required Document:** Form A (GSP Certificate of Origin)

#### Who Issues:

- Government agency (customs/export promotion)
- Authorized chamber of commerce
- In some countries: Self-certification allowed

#### Key Fields:

- Exporter details
- Consignee (UK importer)
- Origin country
- HS code
- Product description
- Origin criteria (wholly obtained/sufficient transformation)
- Issuing authority stamp/signature

### How to Obtain Certificate of Origin (Bangladesh Example)

1. **Step 1: Register:** Export Promotion Bureau (EPB) - [epb.gov.bd](http://epb.gov.bd). Register as exporter.
2. **Step 2: Submit Application:** Online or physical submission. Provide: Commercial invoice, packing list, manufacturer declaration.
3. **Step 3: Inspection (if required):** Some products require physical verification. EPB may inspect manufacturing facility.
4. **Step 4: Issuance:** Form A issued (typically 1-3 days). Digital or physical certificate. Valid for 12 months.

**Cost:** Usually $10-50 per certificate.

### Supporting Documents (Also Required for UK Customs)

- **Commercial Invoice:** Product description, HS code, value (FOB/CIF), origin country. Include "Product of [Country]".
- **Packing List:** Detailed contents, weights/dimensions, marks and numbers.
- **Bill of Lading / Air Waybill:** Routing information, consignor/consignee, proof of direct consignment.
- **Manufacturer's Declaration (if applicable):** Materials used, manufacturing process, origin justification.

## 8. Tariff Savings Calculator

### How to Calculate Your Savings

**Formula:** `Annual Savings = (Export Value × MFN Rate) - (Export Value × DCTS Rate)`

#### Step-by-Step:

1. **Find Your HS Code:** Use [trade-tariff.service.gov.uk](https://trade-tariff.service.gov.uk).
2. **Check MFN Rate:** Look up HS code in UK Global Tariff. Note the "Third Country Duty" rate.
3. **Check DCTS Rate:** Same tool, select origin country, view "DCTS preferential rate".
4. **Calculate.**

**Example:**

- **Product:** Coffee beans (roasted)
- **HS Code:** 0901.21.00
- **Annual exports:** £200,000
- **Origin:** Ethiopia (Enhanced)
- **MFN Rate:** 7.5% | **DCTS Rate:** 0%
- **MFN Duty:** £15,000 | **DCTS Duty:** £0
- **Annual Savings:** £15,000

## 9. Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Top 10 DCTS Compliance Errors

1. **Wrong Certificate of Origin:** ❌ Generic CO. ✅ Form A (GSP).
2. **Insufficient Transformation:** ❌ Minor assembly. ✅ Tariff shift or value-added threshold.
3. **Breaking Direct Consignment:** ❌ Repacking in transit. ✅ Customs control during transit.
4. **Expired Certificate:** ❌ Older than 12 months. ✅ New certificate for each shipment.
5. **Incorrect HS Code:** ❌ Exporter's country HS. ✅ UK 10-digit commodity code.
6. **Missing Cumulation Documentation:** ✅ Provide supplier declarations for cumulated inputs.
7. **Incomplete Invoice:** ✅ Include "Product of [Country]".
8. **Wrong DCTS Tier:** ✅ Verify country's tier before claiming.
9. **No Manufacturer Declaration:** ✅ Keep detailed production records.
10. **Claiming on Excluded Products:** ✅ Check product-specific rules (some have reduced, not zero, rates).

## 10. Step-by-Step Implementation Guide

### For Exporters (DCTS Countries)

#### Phase 1: Qualification (Week 1)

- **Day 1-2: Verify Eligibility:** Check country, tier, HS codes, and calculate savings.
- **Day 3-5: Rules of Origin Assessment:** Map supply chain, identify materials, calculate value-added, verify tariff shift/cumulation.
- **Day 6-7: Documentation Preparation:** Register with authority, prepare declarations, set up record-keeping.

#### Phase 2: First Shipment (Week 2-3)

- **Week 2: Certificate Application:** Submit Form A application, provide documents, pay fees.
- **Week 3: Customs Entry:** Share Form A with UK importer, ensure invoice shows origin, verify routing.

#### Phase 3: Ongoing Compliance (Monthly)

- Maintain records (4 years), update certificates, monitor changes, track savings.

---

### For UK Importers

#### Phase 1: Supplier Qualification

- Verify supplier country, request HS codes, calculate savings, assess compliance capability.

#### Phase 2: Import Setup

- Obtain EORI, set up broker, request Form A, configure documentation.

#### Phase 3: Customs Declaration

- Declare under DCTS code, submit Form A, claim preference in CDS, keep records (4 years).

#### Phase 4: Verification Preparedness

- Maintain declarations, keep production evidence, respond to HMRC, conduct audits.

## 12. FAQs

### General Questions

- **DCTS vs GSP?** DCTS is the UK's scheme (post-Brexit). It is more generous than the old EU GSP.
- **Can I use both DCTS and an FTA?** No. Choose the most beneficial preference.
- **How long does DCTS last?** Indefinitely, with periodic reviews.

### Eligibility Questions

- **My country isn't listed?** Only the 65 listed countries qualify.
- **Northern Ireland?** Yes, but different rules may apply due to the NI Protocol.

### Rules of Origin Questions

- **Chinese components?** Maybe, if sufficient transformation occurs in the DCTS country.
- **Self-certify origin?** Not for DCTS. Form A must be issued by an approved authority.

### Documentation Questions

- **Form A validity?** 12 months from issue date.
- **Retrospective certificates?** Usually yes, within limits (2-3 months backdated).
- **Lost Form A?** Request a certified duplicate marked "DUPLICATE".

### Compliance Questions

- **HMRC verification?** Yes, keep records for at least 4 years.
- **Transit through non-DCTS country?** Allowed if under customs control and not processed.
